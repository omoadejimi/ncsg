## Voice of the guide

- **Direct.** Recommendations are imperatives: "Use sentence case", not "It is recommended that sentence case be considered".
- **Plain.** The guide follows its own [plain language](/docs/foundations/plain-language) guidance. Explain specialist terms where readers need them. Link to a short glossary when it helps people complete a task.
- **Traceable.** Where a recommendation rests on research or standards, the page says so. Where it's a convention chosen for consistency, the page admits that too.
- **Respectful of Nigerian English** as a standard in its own right.

## Page structure

Use this structure when creating or revising guidance:

1. Frontmatter: title, one-sentence description, search keywords
2. Overview — why this topic matters here
3. Principles or guidelines
4. Do and Don't examples (realistic, Nigerian, anonymised)
5. Nigerian-specific considerations, where the topic has them
6. Accessibility considerations, where the topic has them
7. Evidence status, sources and limits
8. Related guidance links

## Examples standard

- Examples use realistic Nigerian names, amounts in naira, and plausible flows.
- Never include real customer data, real account numbers, or identifiable incidents.
- Each Do/Don't pair illustrates **one** principle.

## Evidence and limits

Distinguish the basis of each recommendation:

- **Standard:** link to the exact requirement, version and relevant section. State its scope.
- **Research:** link to a report with the method, date, audience, sample, findings and limitations. A source from another audience does not establish comprehension in Nigeria.
- **Editorial convention:** explain the choice made for consistency. Do not call it user-tested without a linked study.
- **Needs research:** identify an unanswered question and how it could be tested.

Where a page combines these, label the claims separately. A source-check date records a document check, not a completed accessibility review or user study.

For financial, identity and address instructions, record the product or authority, relevant conditions, source-check date and review trigger. Verify fees, codes, limits, data access and return commitments before reuse.

## Reusable evidence record

Record the page or claim, evidence type, source and version, audience, date checked, limitations, responsible reviewer and next review trigger. Leave unassigned roles explicit; do not invent a reviewer or completed sign-off.

## Examples and placeholders

Label fictional scenarios and templates. Use bracketed placeholders for service-specific fees, limits, deadlines and privacy statements. Replace them with verified information before a product publishes the copy. Never present illustrative research counts as real findings.

## Review checklist

Before approving a pull request, check that:

- the recommendation is clear and its scope is stated
- evidence claims link to a source with the relevant limits
- examples are realistic, anonymised and labelled if fictional
- service-specific promises have verified conditions or clear placeholders
- terms agree with related pages
- spelling, headings and labels follow the guide
- internal links, code strings and examples render correctly.

Use a direct, respectful tone. Review unnecessary words in context rather than treating a word list as a substitute for judgement.
